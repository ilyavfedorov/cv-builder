## Executive answer

Tanya should target **hands-on Tech Lead, Principal Engineer, or Staff Engineer roles on mature enterprise .NET/SQL/ERP platforms**, ideally with a small team, no support escalation ownership, no routine on-call, and one clearly bounded modernisation mandate.

This is a better match than a generic Engineering Manager role. It uses Tanya's rare combination of deep SQL and enterprise rules modelling, C#/.NET experience, ERP implementation knowledge, structured problem solving, and proven ability to motivate engineers. It can also reach the salary target: a current Auckland Tech Lead role combining .NET, SQL Server, legacy modernisation and mentoring advertises NZD 170,000-190,000, while 2026 NZ salary data puts Solutions Architects at NZD 150,000/170,000/190,000 across the 25th/50th/75th percentiles. [Robert Half current tech vacancies](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/jobs/all/tech); [Robert Half 2026 Solutions Architect salary](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/job-details/solutions-architect).

The qualification is important: no job title guarantees calmness. Workload, role clarity, control, interruptions, escalation duty and the number of concurrent workstreams must be verified with the hiring manager. The WHO evidence review links high workload, low job control, role ambiguity and role conflict with worse mental-health outcomes; those factors therefore drive the screening model rather than employer adjectives such as "supportive" or "fast-paced". [WHO recommendations and evidence review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK586381/).

## Calm-work screening model

The repository scores each role out of 100:

- Skills fit: 30
- Calm-work fit: 30
- Compensation fit: 20
- Location/work-mode fit: 10
- Energy fit: 10

A high total is not enough if a hard filter fails. Salary below NZD 160,000, excessive office presence, routine on-call or a clearly overloaded role is a no-go.

Positive evidence includes mature products, small teams, clear ownership, bounded domains, stable organisations, explicit flexible hours, no on-call and published work-life practices. Risk evidence includes escalation duty, incident response, multiple squads, presales, workshops, travel, high-growth, greenfield ambiguity, competing priorities and "wear many hats" language.

## Interview questions that expose the real job

1. What percentage of a normal week is scheduled meetings for this person?
2. How many initiatives would I actively own at the same time?
3. Who handles production incidents, customer escalations and after-hours work?
4. How often did urgent work displace planned work in the last three months?
5. How many hours of overtime did the team work in the last six months?
6. Who can reject new work when capacity is full?
7. What are the three outcomes for the first 90 days, and what is explicitly outside the role?
8. Why did the previous person leave, and what made the role difficult?
9. May I speak with a future peer about an ordinary week rather than an ideal week?

## Repository operating model

The existing repository already supports CV extraction, job screening, tailored CVs, cover letters and PDF export through local Cursor skills. Tanya now has:

- `cv-data.json`: structured candidate evidence and preferences.
- `search-profile.json`: hard filters, role families and calm-work signals.
- `job-feed/current-jobs.csv`: current researched opportunities.
- `job-feed/scored-jobs.csv`: repeatable weighted ranking.
- `scripts/job_feed.py`: deterministic importer/scorer for saved-alert exports and bookmarked jobs.

LinkedIn and SEEK should be treated as discovery surfaces, not silently scraped dependencies. Their saved alerts can feed the CSV, while employer career pages or recruiter listings provide the evidence used for screening. This keeps the workflow reliable and reviewable even when platform pages change.

## Limitations and stopping point

Vacancies and availability change quickly. The shortlist reflects pages accessible on 30 August 2026; exact office patterns, salaries and role status must be reconfirmed. Stress scores are inferences from job wording until verified through recruiter or hiring-manager evidence. Australian roles were explored, but many require Australian work rights or local attendance; they should be added only when the employer confirms NZ-based remote employment or contracting.

Research stopped after current salary evidence, occupational-stress evidence, five role families and a dozen live leads converged on the same recommendation. Additional broad searching was unlikely to change the target role hypothesis; the next useful evidence comes from recruiter conversations and job-specific screening.
