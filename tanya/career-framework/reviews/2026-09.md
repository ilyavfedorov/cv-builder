## What the market taught us

- Requirements appearing repeatedly: hands-on .NET/C#/SQL and Azure/platform architecture, technical leadership/mentoring, and the ability to work across an established product or platform. Architecture and data-contract roles also request modern cloud/data-platform depth, but only in roles with unresolved work-design or compensation fit.
- Employers or sectors with credible calm-work signals: established SaaS, small autonomous teams, flexible hours, stated work-life balance, quality and sustainability over shipping speed, a 4.5-day week, mature platforms, and bounded technical ownership. The strongest signal was the established SaaS Tech Lead .NET Developer role (skills/calm/energy all 5).
- Recurring stress risks: on-call and incident response, greenfield or high-ambiguity delivery, broad transformations, competing priorities, presales/workshops and senior stakeholder influence, high-performance wording, and leadership across multiple squads or leaders.
- Salary and work-pattern intelligence: suitable advertised Lead/Staff engineering roles cluster at NZ$165k–190k, with several meeting the NZ$170k preferred floor. One Solutions Architect role advertised NZ$180k–199k but had weak calm fit. Salary, office days, on-call expectations, and contract rates are unknown for many otherwise interesting roles.
- Reasons otherwise attractive roles were rejected: explicit calm-work conflicts (Fusion5: presales, workshops, presentations, cross-sell); explicit on-call/incident load (Trade Me Lead); and broad, high-stakes multi-squad influence (Trade Me Principal). Other roles remain on hold for work-design, rate/salary, office-day, or stack-fit verification.
- Feedback on Tanya's positioning: the market evidence supports leading with hands-on .NET/SQL/Azure technical leadership, mentoring, small-team design ownership, and sustainable product/platform delivery. It does not yet support a broad ERP/presales-architecture or people-management-led positioning.
