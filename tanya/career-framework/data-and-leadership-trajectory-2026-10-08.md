## Priority

1. Search immediately for sustainable leadership roles at or above NZD 160,000, prioritising meaningful proximity to data where available. Keep strong general engineering-leadership roles eligible so a scarce hybrid role does not delay an exit.
2. Explore SQL/analytics roles as a serious parallel direction. Advance only roles meeting income and working-condition gates and whose seniority requirements Tanya can demonstrate.
3. Treat direct data science as a longer option. Do not make retraining a prerequisite for leaving Gentrack.

Initial allocation: 60% search/network/interview effort, 30% SQL/data exploration and one project, 10% research/statistics discovery. One project supports paths 2 and 3. Start with 3–4 hours weekly, not three separate curricula.

## First six weeks

- Weeks 1–2: reconstruct one complex SQL example; establish current Python/statistics skills; have 2–3 conversations with data practitioners/recruiters; rehearse two leadership stories. Ask about approved base salary, actual work mix, production duty and collaboration with scientists.
- Weeks 3–4: use public data to formulate a specific question; inspect quality and grain; produce SQL transformations and a short analysis. Add Python only as needed to extend the investigation. Continue selective leadership applications.
- Weeks 5–6: add reproducibility, validation, limitations and one useful visual; obtain practitioner feedback; rehearse explaining decisions. Decide whether the next learning bet is analytics modelling/tooling or statistical investigation.

Project deliverables: explicit question; data dictionary and source; reproducible SQL; quality checks; small analysis notebook if useful; findings, uncertainty and alternative explanations; five-minute explanation. Work with public/synthetic data, avoiding employer data.

## Questions that distinguish roles

- What share of the week involves SQL/modelling, investigating questions, pipelines/operations, people leadership and meetings?
- Do scientists and engineers jointly shape questions and interpret findings, or do engineers only fulfil data requests?
- Who owns incidents, pipeline failures and out-of-hours work?
- What evidence must a senior hire bring on day one, and what can be learned with the team?
- Is this an established team with technical support, or a sole data hire building everything?

Analytics engineering often focuses on transforming/testing/documenting datasets. Data engineering can focus on pipelines and infrastructure. Neither automatically supplies research time. Leadership can place Tanya near data scientists without giving her hands-on analysis. Verify the ordinary working week, not just the title.
